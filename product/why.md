## memo from drip founder

creators of digital content (data + media) don't get paid, most of the money is made by aggregators (google, fb, etc.) w virtually unbreakable network fx and 90%+ gross margins

this lead to internet ux becoming increasingly bad over last 20 years: paid, ever-increasing subs, paywalls, cookie banners & legalese, intrusive ads as publishers try to wrestle control from aggregators

thesis is the problem stems from lack of micro-payments infra + missing ownership model, two things blockchains solve

micro-payments bc all digital content has an inorganic pricing floor, so bundling is necessary, forcing creators to participate or lose - and, people prefer lower prices, and paying for what they get

digital ownership bc piracy is bad, but licensing and rental models are unfair and expensive for consumers - leaving them unable to opt-out without losing the media they paid for - and, therefore w increasing wallet share going to aggregators, creators that try to break free from the system struggle to monetize

these are things the early internet didn't care about, bc of the enormous subsidies (funded by VCs and public markets), global bandwidth access propagation (default growth keeping shareholders happy without margin expansion imperative), and bc it took a long time for creators and publishers to figure out what was going on

so the time is now to build a better content experience; peak frustration from creators and users, and the invention and readiness of robust, fast, cheap digital payments & ownership infra w Solana et al. that better content experience is DRiP

My favourite part about being a Dean's List DAO citizen is I get exposed to great platforms before everyone else. Sure, anyone can go through the list of hackathon winners and try out the different dapps, but only DL citizens can expect to get their feedback taken seriously and receive compensation for it. That change in expectations helps fuel me to write and share some thoughts on how tools like Sollinked can change the dynamic between Reader and Author

In testing out the Sollinked platform, one of my first thoughts was “How will the platform prevent readers from copying and sharing the material they read here?” One patron could copy/paste the entire article and then thats it – the value created by the Author is out there for free. But ofcourse this could happen with all paywalls. Im not sure how often it happens, but the end result has to be “Who cares!!” The Reader has influence on the future of the Author, and I think that is a good thing.

Today, the paywall is a flimsy obstacle meant to prevent ideas flowing in exchange for patronage. The Reader doesnt have a choice to first test the waters, to see if the content behind the wall is any good and deserving of patronage. I reject virtually 100% of the articles I see with a paywall. Maybe i wouldn't have donated anything after reading it, but at the very least, there was still the possibility of me sharing that content after reading it, and that is a good result to enable.

So how can Sollinked change the existing paywall structure and also evolve past it?

1. A history of the Reader

Sollinked can archive all articles the Reader has read and paid for. This allows the Author to setup different structures for a unique reader/author experience.

Mix this data with other onchain data like grant funding, voting in DAOs, and other activities and we can start to paint a picture of each Reader's civic duty and contributions to the ecosystem.

Communities could focus on attracting avid readers. DAOs could flex that their voters are the most educated on DeFi and tokenomics.

Current paywalls do not do this and are unlikely to ever be able to support this.

2.  On-Demand article requests and a revenue share

Extending beyond the paywall, Sollinked could allow the Reader to request writing from the Author! Any group of readers could pre-pay for content they would want to see. The Author now knows what the Reader group wants to read AND already knows the reward for writing it. There can even be a revenue-share for reads of the new article, giving the Reader group an incentive to patron new ideas and also help market the article they requested!

3. Funding through content

With the ability to create transparent and codified rules, Author's can also share what the funding will go towards. The Reader can have full trust that if they donate to reading an article, a certain amount of those proceeds will go towards specific initiatives they like.

Imagine seeing an article about climate change and knowing that if you pay for the paywall to read it, you know with 100% confidence that a portion of that will go directly to your Climate Change charity of choice.

This could also lead to DAOs that are funded from reading and are controlled by the Reader's themselves!! An article about Cancer research could have different donation tiers, and the highest level could give you voting rights on how the collected funding is used. This gives a new dynamic for paywalls that now makes them content funds.

These are only 3 ideas, but the combination of them creates a new dynamic where the Reader is now joined with the Author. The relationship is no longer one dimensional one transcation, but can create a relationship of responsibility to one another. The Reader now can have incentive to pay, to share, and to contribute ideas that they want to see from the Author. And all of this is only enabled by a transparent and trusted ledger.

If you enjoyed these ideas, please let me know and I promise to write more!
